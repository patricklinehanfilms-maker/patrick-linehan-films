Overview

Song Wish Upon You

The song opens with a piano intro, then the cymbals come in.

Four cameras, four jobs: one on Lily, one on the room, one in the pit, and one on the band.

Together they give the edit close ups of Lily, a safety wide, front row energy, and coverage of the band. All four shoot the same clean look.

We'll capture scratch audio on every camera and record audience sound for the whole show, so the edit has the room cheering and singing along.

The rule for every operator: know your lane. If two cameras are getting the same shot, one of them is wasted.

The cameras

A

Long Lens

Back of room · Long lens · Lily
Operator Jack Cohen

Cam A covers Lily from the back of the room on a long lens. It is the hero angle: tight, steady and compressed.

Position
Back of the main floor, on a riser to clear the crowd, just off center from B.
Framing
MCU to CU of Lily. Let the long lens compress the background.
Lens
70mm to 200mm zoom or longer, depending on the throw.
Rig
Tripod with fluid head, or a monopod. Sandbag it.
Movement
Hold steady. Slow push ins on verses, a touch wider on choruses. Keep Lily's face in focus.
Settings
1/50 shutter at 24fps, native high ISO, log profile. Ride exposure off the face, not the lights.
B

Room Wide

Front of house · Tripod · Zooms
Operator Patrick Linehan

Cam B is the safety wide. It rolls from the first note to the last.

Position
Mezzanine rail, center, looking straight down the room at the stage.
Framing
Full stage plus some crowd as the home frame, with slow zooms into the band and back out.
Lens
Long cine or broadcast style zoom (roughly 24mm to 200mm or longer depending on throw).
Rig
Tripod with fluid head. Sandbag it and tape down cables.
Rules
Never stop recording. Keep zooms slow on the verses, and return to the wide often so the editor always has a cutaway.
Choruses
Crash zooms into the band on the choruses, then back out to the wide.
Bonus
In quiet moments, look for silhouettes, phone lights, and rim lit heads in the foreground.
C

Pit

Front row · Handheld · Lily and the crowd
Operator Jake Z

Cam C works the front row at the lip of the stage: Lily from below and the front row singing along.

Position
Front row at the lip of the stage. Move along the front for new angles on Lily and the band.
No barrier
Bowery has no barricade or photo pit. Get to the front before the room fills and keep the rig tight to your body.
Framing
Two kinds of shots. Lily and the band from below. Front row reactions, turning back to faces singing, lit by stage spill.
Lens
Wide prime (16mm to 35mm). Wide keeps shake tolerable and lets you stay close.
Rig
Small body, handheld with a top handle or cage. No gimbal in a packed crowd.
Etiquette
Keep the camera low when moving and don't block fans for long. Ask before holding on someone's face, and carry release cards.
Crowd
FloRo crowds are fairly tame. Find the front row fans singing every word and stay on them.
D

Side Stage

Side stage · Handheld · Guitar, drums, keys
Operator Micah Bierman

Cam D covers the band from the side of the stage. It shoots the same clean look as A, B and C.

Position
Side stage, off the deck, on the right side of the map. Clear the spot with the tour manager and stay out of crew paths.
Framing
Guitar, drums, keys and the rest of the band: faces, hands on the instruments, the drummer through the kit. Catch the cymbals coming in after the piano intro. Use the crowd behind them for reverses.
Lens
24mm to 70mm zoom.
Movement
Whip pans between players, landing on the beat.
Rig
Handheld, top handle or shoulder rig.
Settings
Match A: 1/50 shutter at 24fps, same log profile and white balance.

B-Roll

Outside · Doors · The city
Shot on A, C and D

B-roll builds anticipation before the piano comes in: the crowd arriving, the room filling, and the city around the venue.

Outside the venue

  • The Florence Road sold-out sign on the marquee, the venue exterior, the line down the block
  • The street and the city around the venue at dusk

Crowd arriving

  • Tickets and wristbands, the merch table, friends taking photos
  • First people crowding the front of the stage

Audience pre-show

  • Fans waiting and singing along to the pre-show playlist, phones up
  • One wide of the room filling up, shot from the same spot at doors and again right before the set

Shoot short, clean clips of 5 to 10 seconds in the same look as the show. No green room shots.
